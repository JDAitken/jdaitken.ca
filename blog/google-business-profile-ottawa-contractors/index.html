How Ottawa Contractors Get Found on Google Business Profile

August 5, 2026 · By JD Aitken · 5 min read

A homeowner in Orleans searches "landscaper near me" and Google shows a map with three pinned businesses before a single website link appears. That map pack is Google Business Profile. If a contractor isn't in it, they're not in the running for that search, no matter how good the website behind door four is.

I manage Google Business Profile for a few Ottawa trade businesses alongside their websites, and the profile usually does more work than the site does for exactly this kind of search.

What Actually Gets a Profile Into the Map Pack

Google ranks the map pack on three things: relevance, distance, and prominence, laid out in Google's own explanation of local ranking. Relevance means the listing matches what was searched, which is mostly a category problem. Distance is mostly out of your control. Prominence is where the real work happens: review count and quality, how complete the profile is, and how often it's posted to and searched for by name.

The category setup catches more businesses than anything else. A landscaping company that filed itself under a vague label like "Contractor" instead of "Landscaper" is competing for a completely different set of searches than the one it actually wants. The primary category should be the single most accurate description of the core business, and secondary categories can cover the rest.

For Legendary Landscaping, getting the categories right alongside a site rebuild put them in the top 3 results for interlocking and hardscape searches in Ottawa, and top 3 for artificial turf specifically in Orleans. That's not one lucky post. That's a profile built to match what the searches actually say.

Reviews Are Doing More Than You'd Guess

A profile with 40 reviews and a 4.8 average beats a website with better photography every time a homeowner is comparing three options on their phone. Review count and recency both factor into ranking, not just the star average, so a business with steady new reviews outranks one sitting on old ones even at the same rating.

Asking for a review right after a job wraps, while the homeowner is still glad it's done, gets a far higher response rate than a generic follow-up email a week later. Responding to every review, good or bad, is a smaller lift than it sounds like and it's one of the few signals a homeowner can see directly when they're deciding who to call.

Posts Aren't Just for Announcements

Google Business Profile posts work differently from a blog post or a social post. They're short, they get pulled directly into search and AI answers when they're phrased like an answer to a real question, and they expire after seven days unless it's an event or offer. That short lifespan is a feature, not a limitation. It signals to Google that the business is active right now, which factors into ranking the same way reviews do.

A post that states the service, the neighbourhood, and a concrete detail, like a price range or a timeline, does more than a generic "we're open" update. J&J Remodeling ranked #1 on Google for "bathroom remodeling" in their first full month live, with search impressions up 981% and clicks up 450%, and none of that came from ad spend. Some of it was the site. A meaningful part of it was a profile that matched exactly what people were typing.

The Part That Surprises People

AI answer engines are starting to pull from the same signals as Google Maps: a complete profile, real reviews, and clear service and area information. One Legendary client mentioned finding the business through ChatGPT rather than a Google search. The profile work that helps a business rank in Maps is increasingly the same work that gets it recommended by an AI assistant, so it's not a separate project to plan for later.

None of this requires a redesign or a big one-time push. It requires someone checking the categories are still right, answering reviews, and posting weekly. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Setting up and managing a listing costs nothing. Paid tools around it exist, like Google Ads or managed posting services, but the profile itself is free to claim and free to update.

How do I rank higher in the Google Maps 3-pack?

Three things move it most: a fully completed profile with the right primary category, a steady stream of reviews with responses, and a website whose service pages match the categories and search terms your profile is competing on. Posting to the profile regularly helps too.

What's the difference between a primary and secondary category on Google Business Profile?

The primary category is the single best description of your core business and carries the most ranking weight, so "Landscaper" beats "Lawn care service" if landscaping is most of your revenue. Secondary categories describe additional services and help you show up for related searches without diluting the primary one.

Does Google Business Profile help if I already have a website?

It helps regardless. A website and a GBP listing rank differently. Some searches, especially ones with local intent like "landscaper near me," pull the map pack ahead of any organic result, so a business with a strong profile and a weak site can still out-compete one with the reverse.

How often should I post on Google Business Profile?

Weekly is a reasonable baseline for a trade business. An inactive profile isn't penalized outright, but a profile that's regularly updated with photos and posts gives Google more current signal to rank on, and gives a homeowner scrolling through search results more reason to trust it.
